# Offers lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Reach is easy.** Buyers are owners in one city, findable through Instagram and 2GIS, and the founder can visit them in person with a demo on a phone. Of the four market tests, reach is the strongest.
- **A real showcase exists.** One restaurant complex (lounge, bar, karaoke VIP rooms) will be the first user. That is where proof can come from.
- **Low effort for the guest.** It opens from a link or QR code with nothing to install. Picking a specific table on the real floor plan is a concrete promise that phone and WhatsApp cannot match.
- **Cheap stack items already exist.** Floor-plan drawing, table photos, joined tables for big groups, and one-tap call or WhatsApp could all be bundled into one offer.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The pain may not be big enough.** Notebooks and WhatsApp work for free. The brief shows no lost revenue: no no-shows, double bookings or missed calls with a cost attached. If the pain is only an inconvenience, buyers will not pay a monthly fee. A weak market is the most common reason this lens passes.
2. **The perceived likelihood is near zero today.** The product stores data on one phone, has no server or accounts, and has no results from a venue. The offer is a list of features, not an outcome with proof behind it.
3. **It sells on features and possibly on price, against established tools.** Russian floor-plan booking SaaS already exists. If the only difference is being local or cheaper, it is a commodity. The offer also asks a lot: the hostess has to change habits, and guests have to stop texting.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- At the showcase venue, how many bookings a week are lost, double-booked or no-shows, and what does each cost in tenge?
- What result does an owner buy: more covers, fewer no-shows, fuller VIP rooms on weekdays? Can you state it as a number?
- How many Aktobe venues take enough bookings to care? (Open question: the count of venues and their booking volume are unknown.)
- How does 19 900 ₸ compare with Restoplace at today's exchange rate? (Open question.) Would a venue pay double for a done-for-you version?
- Who keeps the product running once it is sold, given the non-programmer build and an unknown budget?
- What guarantee could you afford to give?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Move to a real server and accounts, then run the showcase venue live for 4–6 weeks and measure bookings, no-shows and hostess time before and after.
2. Rewrite the offer around a measured outcome, not features. Example: "fewer empty reserved tables, or the next month is free." Back it with a real guarantee.
3. Build the stack as a done-for-you package: floor plan, photos, QR codes printed for tables, setup of Instagram, 2GIS and WhatsApp links, and hostess training. Then test whether the setup fee holds.
4. Get 3–5 paying venues (not free months) before spending more on features.
