# Monopoly lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **A small market that could be owned.** Aktobe is one city of about half a million, and its venues take bookings by phone, WhatsApp and notebook. The brief names no competitor working there today. Starting with a big share of a small market is what the Monopoly lens wants.
- **Distribution has a starting point.** A real showcase venue (a lounge hall, a bar, two karaoke VIP rooms), face-to-face demos and a free first month give a plausible path to the first 10 venues.
- **Local know-how.** Kazakh family-celebration occasions, the +7 phone format, WhatsApp-first contact, 2GIS and Instagram entry points, and karaoke and banquet rooms are local details. A foreign product may handle them badly.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Nothing stops a copycat.** Floor-plan booking is not a breakthrough. Established Russian products such as Restoplace already do it, and the brief gives them as cheaper per month. A POS vendor already in these venues, or Kaspi, could add bookings as a feature. The brief shows no technology 10x better, no network effect, no scale advantage and no brand. "Local and personal" lasts only until a funded player hires a local salesperson.
2. **The ceiling is low and the product cannot be sold yet.** One city's restaurants, lounges and karaoke bars at about 19 900 ₸/month may never add up to a business. The prototype stores data on one phone. The founder does not code, and the budget, available time and plans to hire a developer are all unknown. The window to grab the small market could close before a sellable product exists.
3. **No answer to "why now" and no secret.** If venues have been fine with notebooks, the brief does not show what has changed. It also does not show what the founder knows that Restoplace or Quick Resto do not.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- How many venues in Aktobe would realistically pay? What share must you hold for the business to be worth running? (No number exists yet.)
- Have you checked whether any Aktobe venue already uses Restoplace, Quick Resto or another booking tool? What did they say?
- What is Restoplace's real price in tenge for a Kazakh venue? Why would a venue pay you more?
- What do you know about these venues that an outsider does not?
- What could make you harder to leave each month? For example, a guest-facing Aktobe booking page across venues, guest history, or Kaspi deposits for banquets.
- Who builds and runs the server, and what does that cost per month?
- How many visits and how many days does it take to sign one venue? Does it cost less than a few months of subscription?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Within about 8 weeks, a real server with accounts is live, and the showcase venue runs on it every day.
2. At least 5 other Aktobe venues pay after the free month, and you know what each one cost to win.
3. A written answer on durability: one advantage you will build (for example a guest network across venues, a banquet-deposit flow or a Kaspi tie-in), with a first step already shipped.
4. A local competitor scan: which tools Aktobe venues already use, and at what price in tenge.
